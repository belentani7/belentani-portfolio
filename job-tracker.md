## 5. Interview Prep — Technical Questions

### General Engineering
- Tell me about a project you're most proud of.
- How do you handle technical debt?
- Describe a time you disagreed with a technical decision. How did you resolve it?
- How do you approach code review?

### System Design
- Design a URL shortener like bit.ly.
- Design a real-time notification system.
- Design a distributed cache.
- How would you design a feed system for a social media app?

### Data Engineering (if applicable)
- Explain the difference between batch and stream processing.
- How do you handle data quality issues in a pipeline?
- Design a data warehouse schema for an e-commerce platform.
- What's your experience with [Spark / Airflow / dbt / Kafka]?

### AI/ML Engineering (if applicable)
- How would you deploy an ML model to production?
- Explain the tradeoffs between batch vs. real-time inference.
- How do you handle model drift?
- Describe your experience with LLM integration.

### Behavioral (STAR Method)
- Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly.
- Describe a situation where you had to push back on a deadline.
- Give an example of how you've mentored a junior developer.
- Tell me about your biggest failure and what you learned.

## 6. Salary Negotiation — Barcelona Market

### Market Ranges (2026, Gross Annual — EUR)

| Role | Junior (0–2y) | Mid (3–5y) | Senior (6–10y) | Staff/Principal |
|------|---------------|------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Software Engineer | €32K–42K | €45K–60K | €60K–80K | €80K–100K+ |
| Data Engineer | €33K–43K | €48K–62K | €62K–82K | €82K–105K |
| ML/AI Engineer | €38K–48K | €52K–70K | €70K–95K | €95K–120K+ |
| DevOps/SRE | €35K–45K | €50K–65K | €65K–85K | €85K–110K |
| Backend Engineer | €32K–42K | €45K–60K | €60K–80K | €80K–100K |

> **Note:** Big Tech (Google, Meta, Amazon) can pay 30–50% above local market rates. Remote US-based roles can pay 50–100% more.

### Negotiation Tips

1. **Know your value.** Barcelona is a competitive market but salaries have risen significantly since 2024. Don't anchor too low.
2. **Ask about total compensation**, not just salary:
   - Stock options / RSUs (especially at startups and Big Tech)
   - Performance bonuses (typically 5–15%)
   - Meal vouchers (€150–200/month common)
   - Health insurance (private is standard)
   - Remote work flexibility / hybrid schedule
   - Learning budget (€1K–3K/year)
   - Relocation package (if applicable)
3. **Use competing offers.** If you have multiple offers, you can leverage them (professionally).
4. **Salary ranges exist.** Most companies have a range. Aim for the top 75th percentile if your experience supports it.
5. **Don't give your number first.** Let the company provide the range. If pressed, give a range with your target at the lower bound.
6. **Consider equity carefully.** Early-stage startup equity (stock options) is risky. Use the "4-year vesting, 1-year cliff" framework to value it.
7. **Remote vs. Barcelona adjustment.** Some companies adjust salaries by location. Push back if you're delivering the same value regardless of location.
8. **Review period.** Ask when salary reviews happen. Getting a review after 6 months (instead of 12) can be worth €2K–5K effectively.
9. **Contract type matters.** Permanent contract (contrato indefinido) vs. fixed-term affects benefits. Indefinido is preferred.
10. **Taxes.** Spain's tax system is progressive. Above €60K, the marginal rate is ~37%. Factor this into negotiations.

### Useful Phrases in Negotiation

- *"Based on my research and the value I bring, I'd be looking for a total compensation in the range of €X–Y."*
- *"I'm excited about this role. Can we discuss how the total package compares to the market?"*
- *"I have another offer at €Z. I'd prefer to join you — is there flexibility to match or get closer?"*
- *"What does the equity/stock option plan look like for this role?"*
- *"When is the next salary review, and what does the process look like?"*
